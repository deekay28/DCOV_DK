Acceptance policy

Drawn from the CRITICAL / NON-CRITICAL matrix in the source workbook. A Chinese-origin part in a CRITICAL subsystem is an escalation, not a note.

What a result means
A green result means no Chinese-origin record matched this marking. It is not proof that the part is not Chinese.

Three things can put a Chinese part behind a green banner. Markings can be counterfeited or re-marked, and the package will read as whatever the re-marker chose. A part can be absent from the catalogue entirely, in which case you get grey, not green — but a near-miss marking can fuzzy-match the wrong record. And the catalogue is only as current as its last import.

Of the — records seeded from the source workbook, — have no established country of origin at all. Those return yellow. Yellow is not a soft green; it means nobody has yet done the work to establish where that part was made.

Use this as screening support for a trained inspector: it finds the known-bad fast and it shows its reasoning. It does not clear a part, and it does not replace a teardown.

Matching cascade

Every lookup runs the same six layers, cheapest first, and stops at the first hit. The result screen shows which layer fired and why the others did not. Below 82% similarity nothing is auto-accepted; two candidates within 2 points of each other are reported as ambiguous rather than guessed. Where two records share a marking but disagree on origin, the app refuses the verdict and hands both to you.
